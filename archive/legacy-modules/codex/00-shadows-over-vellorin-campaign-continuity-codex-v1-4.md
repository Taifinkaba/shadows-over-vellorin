## 0. HOW THIS CODEX IS GOVERNED

### Source hierarchy
1. Explicit user corrections/approvals of canon.
2. Events and details explicitly established during actual play, with the later confirmed outcome controlling if the same event was revisited.
3. Earlier campaign character rules and checkpoints.
4. World Bible and NPC Registry **[LOCKED DESIGN]** entries, prospectively only.
5. This codex's **[PROPOSAL]** entries, which do not become canon without approval. Explicitly approved `[GM SECRET — LOCKED]` entries are binding DM truth but remain unknown to Theron until discovered.

**Statuses:** `[CANON]` established; `[GM SECRET — LOCKED]` approved hidden truth; `[LOCKED DESIGN]` prospective setting rule; `[GM SECRET]` confidential design, not character knowledge; `[THEORY]` in-world inference; `[UNKNOWN]` not established; `[PROPOSAL]` awaiting user approval; `[CONFLICT]` inconsistent records; `[RETCON APPROVED]` specifically approved replacement. Every lore claim must carry one of these statuses where ambiguity matters.

**Golden rules:** Do not silently rewrite sword origins, dragon ancestry, dates, party finances, wolf bonds, spell costs, NPC locations or prior outcomes. A successful investigation can establish a *new* fact, not change an earlier fact. No retroactive NPC knowledge, free mana, automatic loot, instant travel, unlimited reserves or involuntary dragon binding. GM secrets must be written before clues that depend on them; if the source history is incomplete, keep them provisional.

### Snapshot vs world rules
A checkpoint is a snapshot, not a universal rule. A prospective travel estimate or new village name is not evidence that Theron already visited it. GM-secret proposals must not be treated as proven lore. Record all edits as `ID | old | new | reason | source | user approved? | effective date`.
